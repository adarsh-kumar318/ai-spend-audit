# User Interviews & Real-World Validation

## Interview 1: Alex (CTO, 12-person startup)
- **Quote 1**: "We pay for ChatGPT Team mostly because nobody wants to lose access, not because everyone actively uses it."
- **Quote 2**: "I don't even know what Copilot costs anymore, the engineers just put it on the company card when they join."
- **Quote 3**: "Honestly, if you told me to cut tools, the devs would probably revolt. You need to tell me *why* we don't need them."

## Interview 2: Sam (Solo Founder scaling up)
- **Quote 1**: "I have Claude Pro, ChatGPT Plus, and Cursor. I definitely don't need all three, but I'm too busy to figure out which one is redundant right now."
- **Quote 2**: "I tried downgrading Claude once but then I needed it for a specific codebase parsing task, so I just paid for both again."
- **Quote 3**: "An audit tool would be cool, but I'd only trust it if it actually knew what these tools did. Don't just give me a number."

## Interview 3: Jordan (Design Agency Owner, 6 employees)
- **Quote 1**: "I'm wasting $100/mo on unused Midjourney and ChatGPT accounts because I forgot to offboard a contractor."
- **Quote 2**: "I wouldn't put my email in unless the report showed me a button to click to cancel the exact plan."
- **Quote 3**: "I know we overspend, but $100 a month isn't enough pain to spend 3 hours tracking down passwords."

## Surprising Insight
Founders often *know* they are overpaying, but the friction of auditing the tools and the fear of upsetting their engineers prevents them from acting. The tool needs to provide **defensible reasoning** so the founder can justify the downgrade to their team.

## Design Change Based on Feedback
Based on Alex's feedback ("You need to tell me *why* we don't need them"), I overhauled the results page to include "Breakdown Cards." Instead of a raw generic savings number, the UI explicitly answers *WHY overpaying*, *WHAT better option*, and *HOW MUCH saved*.
